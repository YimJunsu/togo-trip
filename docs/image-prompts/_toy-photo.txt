RENDER — every image in this set is the same kind of picture.

This is a MACRO PHOTOGRAPH of a physical collectible vinyl figure of the character described
above, posed inside a handbuilt miniature diorama. It is not a drawing, not a painting, and not
a cartoon render of a real place. Someone built this little set on a table and photographed it.

The figure: smooth semi-matte vinyl with a faint clearcoat sheen, gentle subsurface scattering
through the ears, the same face and proportions in every shot. It always wears its woven straw
sun hat unless the scene says otherwise.

PLACE — every set is in South Korea, without exception. Korean architecture, Korean street
furniture, Korean food, Korean vehicles. Never Japanese or Chinese motifs: no noren curtains,
no paper lanterns with foreign characters, no sushi or tempura, no red Chinese lanterns.
Signage, when it appears at all, is small and softly out of focus.

The set: a handcrafted miniature made of real materials at small scale — cut paper, balsa wood,
fabric, wire, printed card, resin water. The craft is allowed to show. Everything is scaled to
the figure, so the whole picture reads as a small built world rather than a full-size location.

Camera: macro lens, slightly below the figure's eye level. The figure is fully sharp; the set
falls off gently behind it. No extreme bokeh, no tilt-shift gimmick.

Light: comes from inside the scene itself — the sunrise, the neon, the streetlamp, the room
lamp. Never a neutral studio softbox. The light source named in the scene is what shapes the
figure, so a dawn scene is warm and low-angled and a night scene is lit only by its own lamps.

Framing: square. The figure is unmistakably the subject and stands about 45% of the frame
height, placed off-centre. The set fills the rest and reaches all four edges — there is no
large empty studio backdrop behind it. Even a bright outdoor scene has built scenery in the
background rather than a blank glow.

BACKGROUND — the set must be dense enough that if you deleted the figure, you could still tell
exactly where this is and what time of day it is. Build the place out of its real props: the
things that only that place has. A thin backdrop with two objects on it is a failure.

NO LETTERING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. This covers all of it: shop signs, destination displays,
menus, screens, posters, labels on bottles and packets, and — most importantly — anything the
figure is holding. An itinerary, a booking slip or a ticket shows only blank paper or abstract
ruled lines and small grey blocks, never words. Signs are dark, blank, blurred or turned away.
A blank sign is correct; invented characters are not.

Never place a logo, brand mark, signature, caption or watermark anywhere in the frame, including
the corners. The picture ends at its edges with nothing written on it.

No people, no second character, no text overlay, no watermark, no border.
