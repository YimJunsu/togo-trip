CHARACTER IDENTITY — "투고트립" mascot quokka.

FIXED — these never change, in any picture. This is what makes it the same character.
Warm caramel-brown fur with a soft cream belly and cream muzzle. Rounded, slightly oversized
head, roughly 45% of total height. Large round dark eyes set wide, with a bright catchlight.
Small rounded ears with dusty-rose inner ears. Round dark button nose. Two soft coral blush
patches on the cheeks. Short arms, plump pear-shaped body, short stubby feet, long tapering tail.
Chibi proportions but not deformed. Readable silhouette.

The muzzle is SHORT and BLUNT — it barely projects from the face, like a soft rounded pad with
the nose sitting on its front. It is never pointed, never snout-like, never pushed forward. If
the muzzle sticks out, the animal stops reading as a quokka and becomes a squirrel or a mouse.

The straw hat is WIDE. Its brim is clearly wider than the head — roughly one and a half times
the head's width — and it sits down on the head so the brim passes just above the eyes and
extends well past the ears on both sides. A small hat perched on top of the skull is wrong.

The head is always large relative to the body and the face is always plainly visible. Never
narrow the head, never lengthen the face, never shrink the eyes.

ACTING — these MUST change from picture to picture. The scene says which.
The mouth, the eyelids, the direction of the gaze, the tilt of the head, the line of the
shoulders and the weight of the stance are all performance, not identity. A quokka squinting
with its cheeks full is the same quokka as one gazing up with its mouth slightly open.

Do not default to the same wide open-mouthed grin every time. That grin is one expression among
many, and using it everywhere makes sixteen pictures look like one picture with the background
swapped. Read the scene's acting note and play that, specifically: half-lidded and slack when
the note says tired, mouth closed and eyes forward when the note says focused, eyes shut when
the note says savouring.

The one limit: this animal is never mean, never smug, never angry. No sharp or furrowed brows,
ever. When an acting note asks for focus, determination or seriousness, play it with a CLOSED
MOUTH and a STEADY FORWARD GAZE — never by lowering, angling or knitting the brows. A frowning
quokka is a failed image: this is a mascot that will be sold as merchandise, and it cannot scowl.
Even its most serious face is soft. Within that, the range is wide — quiet, sleepy, absorbed,
delighted, determined, wistful, amused are all available and all wanted.
